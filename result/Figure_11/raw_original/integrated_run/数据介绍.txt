在以“Start Recording”事件完成视频与网络日志的时间对齐后，分析首先剔除了由垃圾回收（GC）引起的周期性尖峰，并将数据限制在四条通信链路均保持活动的阶段。眼动链路和PC–VR状态同步链路整体较为稳定；较明显的延迟波动主要集中在两条动捕链路。视频对照表明，大多数超过50 ms的延迟区段持续时间不足1 s，主要发生在录制初始化、开始行走、转身以及靠近行人、车辆或障碍物等全身姿态快速变化的阶段。高延迟发生时，动捕监控界面的骨架仍保持连续可见，说明延迟增加更可能源于处理或传输负载升高，而非动捕跟踪丢失。任务完成阶段出现了持续时间更长的延迟升高，并与完成窗口显示及系统进入关闭状态同步，可能与任务状态切换、缓存数据写出和资源释放有关。总体而言，过滤后的结果表现为短暂且与事件相关的延迟增加，而不是持续性的通信链路不稳定。

After synchronizing the network log with the video at the Start Recording event, the periodic spikes associated with garbage collection (GC) were excluded, and the analysis was restricted to the interval in which all four communication links remained active (Figure X). The eye-tracking and PC–VR state-synchronization links remained stable, with 99th-percentile latencies of 5.64 and 40.11 ms, respectively. Higher variability was primarily observed in the motion-capture links, whose 99th-percentile latencies reached 50.30 ms for MoCap-to-PC and 53.69 ms for MoCap-to-VR. Video inspection showed that most latency episodes exceeding 50 ms were brief, generally lasting less than 1 s, and coincided with recording initialization, gait onset, turning, and navigation near pedestrians, vehicles, or obstacles. The motion-capture skeleton remained continuously visible during these events, suggesting increased processing or transmission latency rather than tracking loss. A longer latency elevation occurred during task completion, coinciding with the appearance of the completion dialog and the transition to the closeout state. This behavior is consistent with additional processing associated with task-state switching, buffered-data flushing, and resource release. Overall, the filtered results indicate transient, event-related latency increases rather than sustained communication-link instability.

The street-scene results showed that the motion-capture latency elevations were brief and mainly occurred during gait initiation, turning, and navigation near pedestrians, vehicles, or environmental obstacles, whereas the eye-tracking and state-synchronization links remained stable. This pattern is more consistent with transient contention in the shared motion-capture, scene-processing, and rendering pipeline than with sustained network congestion. For navigation-oriented experiments in which detailed joint kinematics are not required for real-time feedback, the online motion-capture representation could therefore be reduced to the head, pelvis, or body-root pose, while the complete marker or skeletal data are retained for offline reconstruction. The Vicon receiving process should also be decoupled from the Unity rendering loop, with the latest pose sample prioritized for real-time display. On the Unity side, level-of-detail control, occlusion culling, baked lighting, and lower-frequency updates for distant pedestrians and vehicles should be evaluated to reduce transient main-thread contention. These optimizations should be validated through repeated A/B trials using the p95 and p99 latency, the proportion of samples exceeding 50 ms, frame loss, and pose accuracy as joint evaluation criteria.
