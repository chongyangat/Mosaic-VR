After temporal alignment with the onset of video recording, seven periodic and coincident latency bursts on the EyeTracking-to-PC and MoCap-to-PC streams were identified as GC-related disturbances and excluded. The analysis was restricted to the 114.545-s interval during which all four communication links remained active. The eye-tracking and PC–VR state-synchronization links remained stable, with p99 latencies of 4.78 and 36.95 ms, respectively, and no measurements exceeding 50 ms. In contrast, the MoCap-to-PC and MoCap-to-VR links exhibited greater task-related variability, with p99 latencies of 86.32 and 89.98 ms and maximum values of 117.75 and 120.98 ms, respectively. Video inspection showed that the elevated-latency intervals occurred synchronously on both MoCap paths during locomotion and turning near the shelves, repeated reaching and grasping, shelf-to-checkout product transfers, and the task-completion phase. The motion-capture skeleton remained continuously visible throughout these intervals, while the two non-MoCap links were unaffected. These observations indicate transient increases in motion-capture processing and transmission workload rather than tracking loss or sustained communication instability.

The supermarket task produced substantially greater motion-capture tail latency, particularly during reaching, grasping, turning between the shelves and checkout area, and placing products. This result suggests that high-rate skeletal updates may interact with object physics, collision detection, product-state serialization, and interface updates to create short periods of increased processing load. Because upper-limb kinematics are essential for interpreting product manipulation, completely removing the skeleton is not recommended for this task. Instead, the real-time model should retain only the joints required by the experimental hypotheses, disable unnecessary skeletal visualization, and transmit lightweight segment data without additional marker, video, or debug streams. Product communication should be changed from frame-by-frame synchronization of all products to event-driven delta transmission, in which only the identifier and state of the manipulated object are updated. Physics and colliders should remain inactive for static products and be enabled only for the currently manipulated object. Full-resolution marker and skeletal data can still be recorded locally for offline analysis. This multirate architecture would preserve the scientific validity of the reaching and grasping measurements while reducing competition between motion capture, object interaction, and rendering workloads.
